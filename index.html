Offline today, connected tomorrow

Livestock traceability that works where the signal doesn't.

TraceStock is an off-grid livestock traceability and monitoring system for South African farming cooperatives. Smart ear tags, on-farm base stations and cooperative trucks carry every animal's record from remote farms to Red Meat Industry Services (RMIS), the traceability platform that South African buyers and exporters rely on.

The problem

Farmers with full paper records are still locked out of formal markets.

Retailers and export markets now expect each animal's origin, health records and movement history to be online, and RMIS is where the red meat industry looks for them. Other registers, such as the Livestock Identification and Traceability System South Africa (LITS-SA), follow the same model. Many small and medium-scale farmers work in rural dead zones with little or no mobile signal. They keep the records but have no way to upload them.

So they sell through informal channels instead, where prices are lower, buyers are less reliable and regulatory risk is higher.

The Animal Identification Act (No. 6 of 2002) requires all cattle, sheep, goats and pigs to carry a registered brand or tattoo. Digital traceability is not yet legally mandated, but major buyers treat RMIS records as essential.

Traceability exclusion

Without connectivity, records cannot reach RMIS or buyers' systems. A farmer can meet every legal requirement on paper and still be refused by major retailers and exporters.

Unmanaged herd risk

Stock theft goes unnoticed, and diseases such as Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) spread quickly because early signs like a raised body temperature are missed. Whole herds, and a family's livelihood, can be lost.

The solution

Let the cooperative's trucks carry the data.

Farming cooperatives are businesses owned and run by their member farmers. Their offices sit in towns with reliable signal, and their vehicles already visit every farm. TraceStock turns that existing network into a data network: records are captured offline on the farm, picked up by passing vehicles, and uploaded automatically once a vehicle is back in coverage, so every farm's records stay ready for RMIS.

How it works

One animal's data, from pasture to platform

Each hop is store-and-forward, so data is kept until the next link is available.

  1. The ear tag reports

    Every tag sends its identifier (ID) and body temperature to the farm's base station on a regular interval.

  2. The base station acts locally

    With no internet, the Pi records the reading, checks it against health thresholds and pushes alerts to the farmer's phone in real time. Farmers and vets log treatments, dipping, movements and more against the same local database.

  3. A passing truck picks up the outbox

    Everything not yet synced (animals, events, readings, alerts, account changes) is bundled and handed to the cooperative vehicle's propagation node, which stores it on board.

  4. Upload on reaching coverage

    When the vehicle reaches signal, it uploads each farm's bundle to the TraceStock API over Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure (HTTPS) using per-device credentials. The cloud applies it, re-scores compliance and checks for outbreaks.

  5. The reply rides back

    The API's acknowledgement, along with ID mappings, updated user accounts, outbreak warnings and the cooperative's recommendations, returns to the farm on the truck's next visit.

verifiedNothing lost, nothing counted twice

Sync is acknowledgement-based. The farm clears a record only after the cloud confirms it was applied. Anything unconfirmed is resent with the next truck, and the cloud de-duplicates animals by ear tag. Records created offline get local IDs that the cloud maps to permanent ones.

Features

Built for everyone in the supply chain

Five roles share one system. Each role sees only what it needs, on a web dashboard, an Android app, or both.

Android appWebWorks offline

Keep a compliant herd without leaving the farm

  • Herd register with filters by species, age and status, plus a full event timeline for every animal.
  • Record 14 event types: birth, death, retirement, acquisition, vaccination, treatment, vet visit, vital signs and weight, dipping, branding, tattooing, ear tagging, quarantine and movement.
  • Real-time alerts for fever, possible tag detachment or a silent tag, delivered as phone notifications by the base station with no internet needed.
  • Compliance at a glance: outstanding issues, a confidence score and recommendations from the cooperative.
  • One-tap traceability record: a Portable Document Format (PDF) document covering owner, identification, event history, permits and declarations, referencing the Animal Identification, Stock Theft, Animal Diseases and Meat Safety Acts.

Designed for the field

The app tries the farm's base station first and falls back to the cloud, so farmers log in and work the same way online or off.

Large, full-width controls and bigger type on tablets make it usable with working hands, which addresses a problem we identified at the start of the project.

Web dashboard

See every member farm from the cooperative office

  • Cooperative dashboard: non-compliance breakdown by rule, active alerts and confidence per farm, average alert response time, herd population trends and the three farms most in need of attention.
  • "Sync now" pulls whatever the vehicles are carrying and shows each farm's score before and after, with new and resolved violations.
  • Farm compliance: review scores, send written recommendations that reach the farm by truck, and suspend or reactivate farms.
  • Disease monitoring map with 3 km and 10 km risk rings around outbreak farms.
  • Route planner: assign each driver an ordered list of farm stops for the day, using each farm's last sync time.
  • Staff approvals and reporting: approve drivers and managers, and export a farm compliance and monitoring report as a PDF.

Data-driven oversight

Statistical analysis highlights outliers. Farms that resolve alerts unusually slowly, or suspiciously quickly, are flagged using z-scores across the cooperative.

Alert recurrence is compared to the cooperative average, so repeat problems surface early.

Web dashboard

Onboard cooperatives, farms and hardware

  • Set up the hierarchy from cooperative to farmer to farm, with address search to place each farm.
  • Draw farm boundaries on satellite imagery. The geofence's area in hectares is calculated automatically.
  • Node registry: register base stations, static relay nodes and truck nodes, including hardware model and radio settings.
  • One-click provisioning: download a configuration bundle with the device's LoRaWAN-style identity, radio plan and a generated SQLite script that bootstraps the farm's base station.
  • Setup dashboard showing farms that need setup, nodes waiting to sync and nodes silent for over 7 days.
  • Vet registry and approval of new cooperative managers.

Provisioning, done once

Every device gets cryptographically random keys at registration. The truck uses them to authenticate its uploads, and each base station gets its own secret for signing offline sessions.

Android appWorks offline

Treat any animal, on any member farm

  • Consult mode: look up an animal and record vaccinations, treatments, vet visits, vital signs and quarantines.
  • Follow-up tracking: medicated events carry a next-due date, and missed follow-ups appear as compliance issues.
  • Fever response: a temperature spike needs a vet visit within 48 hours, otherwise the farm loses compliance points.

Offline login, anywhere

The vet roster, including securely hashed credentials, is pushed to every base station. A vet can sign in on any member farm even though neither the farm nor the vet has signal.

Android app

Drive the route, and the data travels with you

  • Apply in the app with a driver's licence and selfie, then get approved by the cooperative manager.
  • Today's route on a map with turn-by-turn road geometry, downloadable for use out of signal.
  • No extra work: the propagation node in the vehicle syncs each farm automatically as the truck passes.

Uses what's already there

Cooperative trucks and shared machinery already travel between farms. TraceStock gets a data network from those trips with no new cellular infrastructure.

Under the hood

Engineering highlights

TraceStock is a distributed system built to stay correct while parts of it are offline for days.

Secure farm hub

A Raspberry Pi 4 with its own certificate authority

The base station is real hardware: a Raspberry Pi 4 running the farm's backend in Docker and serving it over its own Wi-Fi hotspot. There is no internet to reach a public certificate authority (CA), so the Pi has its own CA.

  • Transport Layer Security (TLS) everywhere on the farm: the apps talk to the Pi over HTTPS and receive live alerts over a secure WebSocket.
  • Trusted devices: the CA certificate is installed on farmers' and vets' devices, so they can verify the base station.
  • Offline sign-in: credentials synced from the cloud and stored as BCrypt hashes, with JSON Web Token (JWT) sessions that can be revoked.
rule

An 11-rule compliance engine, online and offline

The same rule engine runs in the cloud and on every base station. It checks each animal against South African practice, including species vaccination schedules (FMD, Anthrax, Lumpy Skin Disease, Bluetongue, Pulpy Kidney and more), 14-day dipping and branding deadlines. Each farm gets a confidence score out of 100 that drops with each rule broken and recovers gradually once the issue is fixed.

radar

Outbreak early warning

When 15% or more of a farm's herd is in quarantine, the farm is treated as an outbreak. Every farm within 10 km gets a warning, upgraded to immediate danger within 3 km. The warnings reach those farms on the next truck, even if they have no signal.

The base station checks every reading as it arrives and pushes alerts straight to farm devices:

A traceability PDF that carries the animal's data

When an animal is sold, the seller's traceability record has the animal's full details and history embedded as compressed, machine-readable data inside the PDF. The buyer uploads that PDF, and TraceStock fills in the new animal's profile and history automatically and validates it, even on a base station with no signal.

LoRaWAN-style provisioning

Each node is issued a Device Extended Unique Identifier (DevEUI), a Join Extended Unique Identifier (JoinEUI) and 128-bit app and network keys, plus a radio plan (the EU868 863–870 MHz band, spreading factor (SF) from SF7 to SF10, transmit power and adaptive data rate (ADR)) tuned to its role: base station, static relay or truck.

shield_lock

Security by design

Stateless JWT authentication with server-side revocation, so logging out actually ends a session. Five role-based permission levels, BCrypt passwords with strong-password rules, forced reset of default passwords, and single-use reset links that expire after 30 minutes.

deployed_code

Containerised end to end

Every component ships as a Docker image. The farm stack is cross-compiled to arm64 for the Raspberry Pi. SQLite on the edge is tuned for reliability (write-ahead logging, a single writer), and the cloud runs on PostgreSQL.

Affordable by design

Start small, grow over time

Farms join at the level they can afford and add hardware as they go.

Tier 1

Entry

  • Lowest cost
  • No ear tags required, so events are recorded in the app
  • At least one base station
  • One vehicle propagation node
Tier 2

Standard

  • Bluetooth smart ear tags on animals
  • One base station
  • One vehicle propagation node
  • Any combination between tiers 1 and 3
Tier 3

Full coverage

  • Bluetooth smart ear tags on animals
  • Automated weighbridge and dip scanning
  • Multiple base stations and relay nodes on the farm
  • Propagation nodes across the vehicle fleet

The tiers are reference points, not fixed packages. Any combination in between is supported.
